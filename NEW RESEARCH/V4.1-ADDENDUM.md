## 0. Verification of the research report

Three load-bearing claims were checked against primary sources before use.

| Claim in report | Verified | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Lee & Genovese 1989: distributed helps continuous tasks; discrete "less sensitive" | ✅ | **Understated.** Massed practice *facilitated* the discrete version — a reversal, not insensitivity. |
| Proteau 2005: mixed vision-condition practice hindered video-aiming learning | ✅ | Exact. Larger direction constant error in transfer after mixed practice. |
| Continuous skills retain better than discrete over long delays | ✅ | Bell 1950 (pursuit rotor after 1 year), Ammons 15.5-year retest, Adams 1964/1987 reviews. Also: relearning is faster than original learning (savings). |

One addition the report did not contain: Keetch, Schmidt, Lee & Young (2005) — the
"especial skill" finding. Basketball players practising free throws at seven distances were
disproportionately better at 15 ft than the surrounding distances predicted. **Specificity
holds even inside a single skill family at a single task.** This bears directly on §3.

## 1. Session structure has three timescales, not one **[extends V4 §3.2]**

V4 uses one boundary (`SESSION_GAP_MIN` 60) for everything. The research separates three
processes with different recovery times, and the corpus confirms the fastest one:

| Process | Timescale | Recovers after | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm-up decrement | ~2 min | any rest of a few minutes | `tau_minutes 0.67` (T2b); Adams 1952; pursuit-rotor reminiscence |
| Performance depression from massed continuous practice | grows past 1–2 min unbroken | minutes of rest | Lee & Genovese 1988; Eysenck pursuit-rotor work |
| Fatigue, mood, day-state | tens of minutes to a day | overnight | Forman et al. 2024/2025 (no aiming decline within 30–60 min); Sousa 2020 (2.5 h) |

### 1.1 Two boundaries, one unit

```
WARMUP_EPOCH_GAP_MIN  = 5        // new: warm-up state resets here
SESSION_GAP_MIN       = 60       // unchanged: the statistical unit
```

- **Warm-up epoch** — a gap over `WARMUP_EPOCH_GAP_MIN` starts a new epoch. The §2.2
  elapsed-time exclusion restarts from zero. This is where the reported 10-minute break
  belongs: it resets warm-up, and runs immediately after it will dip then recover above the
  pre-break level (reminiscence). That recovery is performance, not learning, and must not
  read as a jump.
- **Session** — the ICC unit, unchanged. A 10-minute break does not reset fatigue or mood, so
  two epochs 10 minutes apart are still correlated and belong to one session for variance
  purposes. Splitting them would leak clustering across the boundary and understate SE.

### 1.2 The reported "4-minute minimum" and process restarts

A 4-minute minimum maps onto `SESSION_MIN_RUNS` (3) and is the right instinct — a
30-second sitting is not a session estimate.

Process launch/close is not observable from the CSVs. If the app monitors the KovaaK's
process it may use restarts as **epoch** boundaries, since a restart implies a gap. It is
not a better **session** boundary than the gap rule, because a restart 3 minutes after a
close shares state with what came before.

### 1.3 Should the gap be user-determined?

Expose it, but calibrate it. Add to the validator:

```
for gap in [5, 10, 20, 30, 60, 120] min:
  rebuild sessions at that gap
  report median ICC and mean sessions/day
```

The correct `SESSION_GAP_MIN` is where ICC stops rising. Show that curve; let the user set
the value against it. T8 already shows the 60-minute boundary sits in an empty band (0.53%
of gaps in 30–120 min), so it is unlikely to move much for this user, but the curve makes
the choice evidence-based rather than asserted.

### 1.4 How this fits the research on trial length

The report's conclusion — 60 s is fine, the lever is rest *between* runs — is a statement
about **practice quality**. Session boundaries are a statement about **statistical
independence**. Different questions. The one connection is §1.1: because distributed
practice helps continuous tasks and post-rest recovery is expected, post-break runs must be
warm-up-corrected as a fresh epoch, or the recovery reads as improvement.

## 2. V4 conflates familiarisation with forgetting **[corrects V4 §2.3, §8]**

### 2.1 The error

V4 §8 fits familiarisation on **cumulative runs**:

```
score(k) = A - B * exp(-(k + E) / lambda)      k = cumulative runs on this cell
```

This is monotone in `k`. A cell with 200 runs in 2024, a six-month gap, and a run today has
`k = 201`, and the model says the player is at asymptote. They are not.

V4 §2.3 "re-familiarisation" was an attempt to patch this with a post-gap drop, but it
treats the effect as noise to discard rather than as a process to measure. **Familiarisation
is a function of cumulative practice. Forgetting is a function of elapsed time.** They are
different processes with opposite inputs and must be modelled separately.

### 2.2 What the retention literature establishes

- Forgetting increases with retention interval (Ammons; Adams 1987).
- **Relearning is faster than original learning** — savings — so a return curve has a
  shorter time constant than the initial curve.
- **Continuous skills retain far better than discrete ones.** Pursuit rotor performance
  survives a year (Bell 1950) and is measurable after 15 years (Ammons). Discrete
  button-pressing skills decay much faster. The continuous/discrete split predicts retention
  better than implicit/explicit does.

That last point yields a testable prediction for this corpus: **tracking scenarios will show
smaller retention loss after long gaps than clicking scenarios.**

### 2.3 The fix — measure retention, do not model it

Retention functions have parameters (floor, decay constant) that vary by task type and
person, with no calibration available. Do not fit them. Instead, treat every long-gap return
as what MEASUREMENT-SPEC §5.3 already says it is: **a naturally occurring retention test,
and the purest Skill measurement the data contains.**

```
per cell:
  gap_days = days since the previous run on this cell

if gap_days > RETURN_GAP_DAYS:
  mark the next RETURN_BLOCK_N runs as a return block
  fit score(j) = A_ret - B_ret * exp(-j / lambda_ret)     j = runs since return
  report:
    A_ret               retained level
    lambda_ret          relearning speed
    A_ret / A_before    retention fraction        <- the Skill number
    lambda_ret / lambda retention savings
  set k_eff = the k on the original curve where A(k_eff) = A_ret
  resume the familiarisation curve from k_eff, not from k_cumulative
```

**Do not exclude return runs.** They are depressed for Form purposes but they *are* the Skill
measurement. Route them to the cold-start series (§5.3) and exclude them only from the
period's Form level.

The `k_eff` reset closes the loop: after a return, the cell's position on the familiarisation
curve is recalibrated to its measured retained level, and savings falls out automatically —
the re-climb starts from `k_eff > 0`, so it is faster than the initial climb without a
separate savings parameter.

### 2.4 Calibrating `RETURN_GAP_DAYS`

14 was a placeholder. Forgetting is continuous, not a step, so any threshold is a detection
convenience. Calibrate it: for each gap length, compare the first post-gap run to the
pre-gap level. `RETURN_GAP_DAYS` is where the first-run deficit becomes distinguishable from
the ordinary cold-start deficit (92.62%). Below that, a gap is just a cold start; above it,
retention loss is measurable on top.

Add to the validator: first-run-after-gap as % of pre-gap level, binned by gap length
(1–3d, 4–7d, 8–14d, 15–30d, 31–90d, 91+d), split by task type.

## 3. Scenario families — the model, and what it needs **[new; extends V4 §6, §9, §10]**

### 3.1 The problem stated correctly

V4 treats every (scenario × cm) cell as isolated. A cell with 100 runs and 100 related cells
with 10 runs each share no information. That wastes most of the corpus and is the mechanism
behind "0 of 70 measurable."

The temptation is to pool related scenarios. The research says pooling is dangerous:
Proteau's specificity findings are for aiming specifically, and Keetch et al. show
specificity *within* a skill family. **So neither isolation nor pooling is correct. The
answer is partial pooling** — the standard hierarchical-model result, and the natural
extension of the inverse-variance weighting V4 §6 already uses.

### 3.2 The model

Within a family, cell-level changes are treated as draws around a family-level change with
between-cell heterogeneity `tau²`:

```
change_i  ~  N( mu_family,  SE_i² + tau² )

tau²       by DerSimonian–Laird
mu_family  = sum(w_i * change_i) / sum(w_i),   w_i = 1 / (SE_i² + tau²)
SE_family  = sqrt(1 / sum(w_i))
I²         = (Q - df) / Q,   Q = sum( (change_i - mu_fixed)² / SE_i² )
```

Each cell's own estimate is then **shrunk toward the family mean** by an amount that depends
on how little data it has:

```
shrink_i   = tau² / (tau² + SE_i²)
change_i*  = shrink_i * change_i + (1 - shrink_i) * mu_family
```

A cell with 100 runs has small `SE_i`, `shrink_i` near 1, and keeps its own value. A cell
with 5 runs has large `SE_i`, `shrink_i` near 0, and is pulled to the family. This is the
"borrow strength" mechanism, and it is exactly what the 100-runs-plus-100-related-cells case
needs.

### 3.3 `I²` is the self-correction

`I²` measures how much of the between-cell variation is real disagreement rather than noise.
**High `I²` means the family is not behaving as one skill** — the cells are moving
independently and should be split. Low `I²` means the grouping is sound.

This is how specificity gets measured rather than assumed. If Proteau and Keetch are right
for this user, loosely-grouped families will show high `I²` and tight difficulty tiers will
show low `I²`. The data adjudicates how narrow transfer actually is, per family, per user.
Report `I²` beside every family estimate and flag families above `I2_SPLIT_THRESHOLD` (75%).

### 3.4 What the model needs from the user

Two things, and one is optional. The proposed similarity ladder (easier / slightly easier /
almost equal / … / similar / semi-similar / different) conflates them and should be split:

```json
families.json
{
  "smoothsphere_viscose": {
    "type": "tracking",
    "members": [
      { "scenario": "Smoothsphere Viscose Easier", "tier": 1 },
      { "scenario": "Smoothsphere Viscose",        "tier": 2 },
      { "scenario": "Smoothsphere Viscose Hard",   "tier": 3 },
      { "scenario": "Smoothsphere Viscose Expert", "tier": 4 }
    ]
  }
}
```

- **`members`** — required. Which scenarios are the same underlying skill. Binary: in or out.
- **`type`** — required. `tracking` (continuous) or `clicking` (discrete), with `switching`
  and `mixed` as needed. **This is the variable the research says matters most** — it
  moderates distribution effects (§1), retention (§2), and fatigue. See §4.
- **`tier`** — optional. Ordinal difficulty within the family. Enables §3.6.

**Do not model cross-family similarity** ("similar but different") in the first version.
Specificity says start tight; `I²` will say whether to loosen.

### 3.5 Seeding families from names

Name parsing is a seed the user confirms, not a source of truth.

```js
const TIER_SUFFIX = /\s+(easier|easy|normal|medium|hard|harder|expert|novice|
  intermediate|advanced|small|smaller|large|larger|micro|macro|fast|slow|
  \d+°?|v\d+|s\d+)\s*$/i;
family_stem = scenario.replace(TIER_SUFFIX, '').trim();
```

Strip repeatedly until stable. Show the resulting groups; let the user merge, split, and
assign tiers. This handles `Smoothsphere Viscose {Easier,∅,Hard,Expert}` cleanly and fails on
`1w2ts / 1w4ts / 1w6ts` or `Pasu Track / Air Angelic`, which the user resolves by hand.

For benchmark suites, hardcode the definition. A suite with 1:1 tier correspondence is a
family per row with `tier` set from the suite's difficulty level. A few dozen entries, not
60,000.

### 3.6 What difficulty tiers unlock

**The challenge-point diagnostic.** With tiers, the app can see where the user's learning
edge is:

```
per family, per tier:  is the familiarisation curve at asymptote, climbing, or below baseline?

tier 2 at asymptote, tier 3 climbing        -> tier 3 is the challenge point; train there
tier 3 flat, tier 4 below its own baseline  -> tier 4 is past it; regress to 3
all tiers at asymptote                      -> family is exhausted; rotate it out
```

This makes the research report's "regress to an easier variant when past the challenge
point" computable instead of advisory.

**Scores are never compared across tiers.** Each cell is normalised to its own history first;
only *changes* are pooled. A 6% gain on Hard and a 6% gain on Normal contribute equally to
`mu_family`; the raw scores are on different scales and stay separate.

### 3.7 Where families sit in the hierarchy

```
cell (scenario × cm)     -> too fragmented; the input
family                   -> partial pooling; new intermediate level
benchmark                -> fixed basket, external threshold; primary headline
free aggregate           -> everything; secondary
```

Family is the level at which "0 of 70" starts to move. A family of four tiers with 25 runs
each has the effective data of one 60–80 run cell, depending on `I²`.

## 4. Task type is a first-class field **[new; extends V4 §1, §3, §9]**

Three verified results depend on whether a scenario is continuous (tracking) or discrete
(clicking):

| Effect | Continuous | Discrete | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distributed vs massed practice | distributed better | **massed better** | Lee & Genovese 1989 |
| Long-delay retention | high (years) | low (weeks) | Adams 1964; Bell 1950; Ammons |
| Within-bout decrement | grows past 1–2 min | negligible | pursuit-rotor literature |

V4 has no task-type field. Add it to `families.json` (§3.4) and use it in:

- **§1** — warm-up epoch handling. Post-rest recovery is expected for tracking; for clicking,
  a break may *cost* rather than help, so the epoch correction has opposite sign expectations.
- **§2** — `RETURN_GAP_DAYS` calibrated separately per type. Expect a longer threshold for
  tracking.
- **§3** — never pool across types. A family is one type.
- **Validator** — split T2b, T3, T10, and the new retention test by type. The corpus has
  enough of both to measure the split.

Task type is inferable from the file — scenarios with kill rows are clicking, those without
are tracking (PARSER-SPEC §1.2). Seed it from that; confirm in `families.json`.
